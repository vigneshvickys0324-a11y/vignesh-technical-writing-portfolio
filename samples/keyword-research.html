A Beginner's Guide to Keyword Research

Find the topics your audience cares about and build content around their needs.

Audience: New bloggers, marketers, and small business owners

What is keyword research?

Keyword research is the process of identifying the words and phrases people use when searching online. It helps content creators understand audience questions, discover relevant topics, and decide which pages to create or improve.

Effective keyword research is not simply about choosing phrases with the highest search volume. The best opportunities are relevant to the audience, aligned with the website's purpose, and supported by content that genuinely answers the query.

Step 1: Understand your audience

Begin by identifying your readers, their goals, and the problems they want to solve. A software company targeting first-time users may need beginner tutorials, while an experienced technical audience may prefer detailed implementation guides.

Write down common questions from customer conversations, support requests, sales discussions, and product feedback.

Step 2: Build a seed keyword list

Seed keywords are broad phrases that describe your subject. For a project management product, examples might include:

  • Project management software
  • Task tracking
  • Team collaboration
  • Project planning templates
  • Workflow automation

Use these starting points to discover more specific questions and related topics.

Step 3: Explore keyword ideas

Keyword research tools can provide estimates of search volume, competition, related phrases, and trends. Search engine suggestions and discussions in relevant communities can also reveal how people describe their problems.

Treat tool metrics as estimates, not guarantees. A phrase with modest search volume may still be valuable when it attracts the right audience.

Step 4: Identify search intent

Search intent is the reason behind a query. Understanding it helps you choose the right content format.

  • Informational: The reader wants to learn something, such as "what is workflow automation?"
  • Navigational: The reader wants a particular website or product.
  • Commercial investigation: The reader is comparing possible solutions.
  • Transactional: The reader is ready to take an action, such as signing up or purchasing.

A useful article should satisfy the reader's intent instead of forcing unrelated product promotion into the page.

Step 5: Evaluate relevance and feasibility

Review each keyword for relevance, intent, potential value, and the effort required to create a strong page. Consider whether your website has enough expertise and evidence to compete for the topic.

Do not select a topic solely because a tool reports low competition. Confirm that it matters to your audience and supports your content goals.

Step 6: Group keywords into topics

Group related phrases by the problem they address. Instead of creating separate articles for every minor wording variation, one comprehensive page may answer several closely related queries.

Use supporting pages for genuinely different questions and link them together where it helps readers navigate the subject.

Step 7: Create a content brief

Before writing, document the primary topic, target audience, search intent, key questions, proposed headings, supporting sources, and intended next step. This brief keeps the article focused and makes review easier.

Common keyword research mistakes

  • Choosing keywords based on volume alone.
  • Repeating keywords unnaturally throughout an article.
  • Creating multiple pages that compete to answer the same query.
  • Ignoring the questions readers actually need answered.
  • Failing to review content performance after publication.
Practical tip: Choose one clear audience problem, research how people describe it, and create the most helpful page you can. Relevance and usefulness should guide the process.

Conclusion

Keyword research connects audience needs with content planning. By combining topic relevance, search intent, careful research, and useful writing, you can create content that serves readers and supports a sustainable SEO strategy.
